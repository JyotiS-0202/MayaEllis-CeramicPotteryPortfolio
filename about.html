I’m Maya Ellis, a ceramic artist based in Surrey, British Columbia. I work with clay to create functional pottery and decorative pieces that bring handmade details into everyday spaces.

My practice combines wheel throwing, hand-building, and surface exploration. I enjoy moving between these techniques because each offers a different way to develop an idea. The wheel encourages rhythm and balance, while hand-building gives me freedom to My Creative Process and create less regular forms.

My Background

My interest in ceramics began during an introductory pottery workshop. I was drawn to the way clay responded to small movements and how much concentration a simple form could require.

That first experience encouraged me to study ceramic techniques and spend more time practising in a shared studio. Through repetition, guidance, and experimentation, I developed a stronger understanding of shaping, finishing, and glazing.

Today, I continue to build my practice through independent projects and small collections. Each project gives me an opportunity to refine familiar skills and explore a new direction.

What Inspires My Work

I often find inspiration in things that are easy to overlook: the rounded edge of a stone, marks in weathered wood, or the changing colour of a landscape.

These observations influence my forms and surfaces. A vessel might borrow its proportions from a natural shape, while a glaze combination might reflect the colours of sand, water, or earth.

I am also interested in how people use objects. Thinking about the person who will hold a mug or serve a meal helps me make decisions about size, weight, and comfort.

My Skills and Approach

Wheel Throwing

I use the pottery wheel to create mugs, bowls, and vessels with balanced forms. I pay attention to wall thickness, proportion, and the way each piece sits on its base.

Hand-Building

Pinching, coiling, and slab construction allow me to explore shapes beyond the wheel. I use these methods for decorative pieces and forms that benefit from a more individual silhouette.

Surface Development

I experiment with carved lines, pressed textures, and glaze combinations. These details can emphasise the shape of a piece or introduce contrast between smooth and textured areas.

My Creative Process

1. Observe and Sketch

I begin by gathering references and making quick sketches. I consider the purpose of the piece, its proportions, and the details I want to explore. Sketching helps me compare ideas before working with clay.

2. Shape and Experiment

As the clay is still soft, I develop the form on the wheel or build it by hand. I often make more than one version so I can compare shapes and see which approach works best.

3. Refine the Details

As the clay becomes firmer, I trim bases, refine edges, and attach handles where needed. This stage helps me improve the balance and finish of the piece.

4. Test the Surface

I use glaze samples and small test pieces to explore colour and texture. Testing helps me choose a finish that supports the form and reveals the details I want to highlight.

5. Fire and Reflect

After firing, I review the finished piece. I consider what worked, what changed, and what I would adjust in another version. These observations become useful starting points for future work.

Experience and Future Direction

My experience includes developing small ceramic collections, working in shared studio environments, and helping with beginner pottery sessions. These experiences have strengthened both my practical skills and my ability to explain a process clearly.

I would like to continue exploring larger vessels, more detailed surfaces, and collaborations with other makers. My goal is to build a body of work that remains useful, personal, and open to experimentation.
