Selected Ceramic Work

This portfolio brings together functional collections, decorative vessels, and experimental studies. Each project explores a different relationship between form, surface, and use.

Across my work, I return to simple shapes and thoughtful details. I want each piece to feel considered, whether it is used every morning or displayed as a decorative object.

Everyday Collection

Functional Pottery | Wheel-Thrown Stoneware

The Everyday Collection explores the objects people reach for most often. It includes mugs, cups, and breakfast bowls designed around comfort and regular use.

Design Process

I began by studying how different shapes feel in the hand. I tested handle curves, rim thicknesses, and the proportions of each piece. These changes helped me develop forms that feel balanced and comfortable.

Glaze Application

The collection uses cream, muted blue, and warm brown glazes. Selected areas of exposed clay create a contrast in texture and connect the finished pieces to their original material.

Reflection

This project taught me how small adjustments can affect the experience of using an object.

Gather Collection

Tableware | Serving Bowls and Platters

The Gather Collection was developed around the idea of sharing food. I wanted to create pieces that could hold a generous serving while remaining easy to carry and arrange on a table.

Design Process

I explored wider forms, shallow curves, and simple edges. The shapes allow the food to remain visible, while the glazed surfaces add colour to the setting.

Reflection

Creating larger pieces encouraged me to pay closer attention to structure and drying. I refined the collection through several versions, adjusting the proportions and weight.

Intended Use

The finished pieces are intended for everyday meals, celebrations, and the conversations that happen around a shared table.

Quiet Corners

Decorative Ceramics | Hand-Built Vessels

Quiet Corners is a series of vases and decorative vessels inspired by stones, hills, and natural surfaces. The project focuses on how a ceramic object can bring interest to a small area of a room.

Design Process

I used hand-building techniques to explore rounded bodies, narrow openings, and slightly irregular outlines. These forms allowed me to move away from the symmetry of wheel-thrown pottery.

Reflection

Matte finishes and subtle textures support the shapes without overwhelming them. Some pieces include carved marks, while others rely on the movement of the surface itself.

Intended Use

This collection helped me become more confident in allowing the character of the making process to remain visible.

Surface Studies

Experimental Work | Texture and Glaze Tests

Surface Studies is an ongoing project exploring how different marks interact with glaze. I create small tiles and vessels using carved lines, stamped patterns, and pressed impressions.

Applying similar glazes to different textures allows me to compare the results. A glaze may collect in a carved area, soften an edge, or make a shallow pattern more noticeable.

Reflection

I record these experiments so I can return to successful combinations when developing new work. Even a result I do not use can help me understand what to change.

These studies are a practical part of my process and a space where I can experiment freely.
